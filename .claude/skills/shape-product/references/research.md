# Research that can change the decision

Use for external comparisons, unfamiliar capabilities, or consequential bets. Routine fixes and settled choices need no literature review.

Start from the decision and its uncertainty. Choose relevant source families before searching:

- Direct predecessors, upstream projects, dependencies, and named inspirations. Check current releases and actual source, not remembered positioning.
- Maintained alternatives and practitioner implementations, including issue reports and failure paths. A feature being shipped establishes availability, not effectiveness.
- Independent measurements and credible counterevidence. Check task selection, models, budgets, human effort, and whether results transfer to this job.
- This product's users, existing work, and observed failures. Outside popularity cannot replace evidence from the affected job.

Follow dependencies or adjacent approaches when a finding exposes another gap. Search for evidence that could overturn the leading recommendation; newer or vendor-authored is not automatically stronger. Verify relevant versions and paper revisions. Distinguish implemented behavior, executed results, author reports, and your inference.

Return the recommendation, what was checked, important omissions, and what would change the decision. Keep useful citations and version pins in the existing work record or chat. Do not create a source quota, recurring scan, or research artifact tree. Stop when remaining uncertainty cannot change the bounded decision; name the limit instead of claiming exhaustive coverage.
