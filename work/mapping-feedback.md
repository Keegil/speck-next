# What the first mapping taught the kernel

**Outcome:** mapping stops being a document the agent hands the owner and becomes a conversation the owner steers, with a place for the decision that currently has none. Four changes, each in its smallest form: a `map-build` skill parallel to `shape-product`, with a question set, numbered rounds carrying the owner's answers verbatim, and a stated stopping condition · a named home for the substrate decision — what the product runs on — with the pieces' requirements as its input, decided once, at a stated care level · a mechanical completion test for a map · and a defined route from a later finding back into `product.md` and `decisions.md`, the mirror of the ruling trace that already runs forward.

**How I'll know it works:** the owner steers the next mapping the way he steered shaping — visible rounds, his words on record, and no moment where he has to ask what process is running. Measured on the next campaign that maps: the map's completion test is run rather than asserted; the substrate decision appears in its own place with its own care level instead of inside the mapping conversation; and at least one finding from a later phase travels back into a promise through the named route with a trace at both ends.

## The evidence: the bridge's own prediction was tested, and half of it failed

[work/build-transition.md](build-transition.md) staked its proving run on Pilot's next build day and named the judge: *"the owner doesn't ask where we are, and nothing feels aimless."* That run happened on 2026-08-17 and 2026-08-18. The artifact half passed. The process half failed, in the owner's own words: *"this first round of mapping also seems extremely unstructured and haphazard. I don't really have a feeling that you are following a process and ticking off the right boxes."* Compared, unprompted, against the phase that worked: *"I think shaping worked really well."*

**What passed, and should not be touched.** `map.md` as a separate artifact earned its place immediately. Nine ordered pieces, one live, every drawn frame and every named promise assigned. Three decorrelated reviewers pointed at three different references returned eighteen findings, five promise-breaking, four of them structural — including a ruling the map had reversed (the owner had been offered "BYOK-only start" in shaping round 13 and picked against it; the map reinstated it and scheduled the pay door last, which also locked out the households the later pieces need to be proven on). The unconsumed-material law caught three shaping documents with no home. Shaping's outputs as the build's inputs is a good law and it held.

**What failed is that mapping has no conversation and no rounds.** `shape-product` is explicitly an owner conversation with a question set; the kernel's map paragraph describes what `map.md` *contains*, not how anyone arrives at it. So mapping ran as authorship: the agent wrote a document, reviewed it, revised it, and handed the owner prose. Real work happened underneath — frame arithmetic, promise coverage, three fresh-context refutations — and none of it was legible to the owner as progress, because shaping's legibility came from numbered rounds with his answers recorded and mapping has no equivalent. The complaint is precise: not that the work was absent, but that the process was invisible. Mapping decides what gets built, in what order, and what proven means for each piece. That is the second most consequential conversation in a product's life and the kernel currently casts it as a file.

**And the substrate has nowhere to live.** The mapping session ran: map written → reviewed → revised → then a stack question → a nine-agent verification → an all-cloud design → the owner rejecting it as overbuilt (*"I think we went a bit overboard with the GCP nativeness"*) → a narrower one. None of that sequence appears anywhere in the kernel. What a product runs on is decided between mapping and building, it is consequential, it is expensive to reverse, and it has no artifact, no gate, no care level and no conversation shape — so it surfaced inside mapping and dissolved mapping's edges. Some of that sprawl is the agent's; the hole is the kernel's, and it will reappear in every greenfield campaign.

**The completion test was invented locally and worked.** Every promise token in `product.md` assigned to a piece, every drawn frame assigned exactly once with no gaps or duplicates, everything unconsumed listed. Run rather than asserted, it caught a dropped persona, three of four bans with no accounting row, a frame name that had drifted from its own caption, and three shaping documents neither consumed nor listed. The kernel should own this rather than each repo re-inventing it.

**The back-path was improvised.** Substrate research produced findings that changed product promises: the engine retains prompts for up to ninety days when a classifier fires, so the data screen gained a sentence; deletion leaves weeks of residue in any backup, so `«Slett alt»` became a per-household key destruction; and "EU region" turned out to be an at-rest-only commitment outside the model endpoint, which changes what the honesty screen may claim. Later-phase evidence rewriting shaping-phase artifacts is exactly the seamless movement the owner is asking for. It worked, and only because the agent improvised it. The kernel traces rulings forward into work; nothing traces findings backward into promises.

## The receipt law is bitten twice, not once

This repo's `state.md` carries the open strain: the receipt law had a scope blind spot, every adversarial pass of the Pilot shaping campaign ran receiptless, the shaping-review extension closed the hole, and it stays *"unproven until Pilot's next pass shows receipts from pass one."*

That pass has now run — the three map reviewers, 2026-08-17 — and it produced a summary, not a receipt. Reviewer identity, model and session were not recorded at dispatch, the exact commands were not captured, and the findings were paraphrased into `state.md` rather than linked. A receipt reconstructed afterwards is not the artifact the law asks for, and reconstructing one is what the builder does when nothing forced the capture at the time. So the fix has not taken: the law exists, the summons is structural, and the *capture* is still discretionary. Worth considering whether the receipt has to be written at dispatch rather than at closure.

## Open

Unproven, by construction: this is a report from one campaign's first mapping, and every claim in it is about that campaign. The four changes are the owner's calls, made 2026-08-18 after reading the diagnosis; their shapes are not designed yet, and the smallest-form discipline applies to each — the kernel has added exactly one artifact class in its life and should be reluctant to add a second. The `map-build` skill is the one that risks it: if the substrate's home turns out to be a section of the map rather than a place of its own, that is one fewer thing to carry.
