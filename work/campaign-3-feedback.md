# What the third campaign taught the kernel

**The campaign:** the pilot's second piece — a headless, AI-first substance engine on the founder's real data. Five adversarial rounds (two full hearings, each with two experiencers and two independent judges, plus an outsider's 68-clause charter read), two route-backs, a convergent LAND — and then the owner's first product-grade look at the output reopened shaping in one message. The primary record lives in the product's own private repo; this file carries only what generalizes.

**Outcome sought:** the reference failure that cost this campaign five rounds cannot happen to the next one — the hearing's grading reference, the roster's composition, and the owner's named priorities each get one small law.

**How I'll know it works:** at the next campaign's first hearing, one experiencer's record cites only `product.md` and the rendered output, never the charter; a works verdict names the run that earned it; and an owner sentence naming "the key" visibly re-sequences the map in the same session.

---

## What the campaign proved first

The hearing machinery held under its heaviest fire yet: route-backs converged, judges re-verified findings in source and ordered runs where evidence was thin, the second-judge rule produced independent convergence every round, and the literal-Built gate plus receipt-at-dispatch held. The product's truthfulness is real — five independent recounts to the digit, the household's word beating everything, degradation that never lies. **None of that is the lesson. The lesson is that all of it can be true of a product that misses its jobs.**

## Gap 1 — the hearing's reference was the charter, and the charter is the builder's

Five rounds graded «delivers the promise» against the piece's own charter. The charter promised honest machinery, so the hearings perfected honest machinery — every verdict citing records, every record citing the charter, the whole loop closed on a corpus one author wrote. The owner then graded the OUTPUT against the jobs and found it missing the mark in five minutes, using the one reference no roster ever held: `product.md`. This is the reference-decorrelation axis, unenforced.

The kernel's form, two laws in the hearing:

- **«Delivers the promise» is graded against `product.md`'s promises and jobs — the piece's charter is never that verdict's reference.** The charter says what was built; the product file says what was promised; only the second can catch a charter that drifted.
- **Every hearing's roster includes at least one experiencer whose only references are `product.md` and the piece's rendered output — banned from reading the charter.** A roster that reads only the corpus cannot find a corpus that drifted from the brief.

## Gap 2 — headless is not tasteless: every piece's output gets rendered and graded

The piece declared «no consumer surface exists while it is live», and that sentence quietly exempted its OUTPUT from taste. Experiencers judged the harness and the boards — «good to use» was ruled about instruments. The kernel's form, one law in the piece loop: **whatever a piece produces, its hearing renders that output at the owner's altitude and grades it against the jobs it serves.** A piece whose only surfaces are instruments gets its output rendered anyway — a page, a printout, anything the owner could look at — because the owner's five-minute look at a rendered page caught what ~15 fresh contexts missed.

## Gap 3 — a works verdict requires a persona that RAN the real path

Both household-facing falsehoods this product ever produced (the dead-habit assertions of hearings one and three) came from ordinary live derivations and were found by reading their output. Attack-only personas — no provider calls, hand-built inputs — are structurally capped at what the gates can be talked past into; one ruled «works: yes» while incapable of reaching the failure mode the verdict is about. The kernel's form: **a works verdict cites at least one run of the real path against the real dependency, or it says in as many words that it is a verdict about the gates.**

## Gap 4 — a mid-hearing fix carries a judge-executable pre-fix control

Fixing walker findings mid-hearing was the right call twice — and it cost the hearing its single-subject property. The final judge's rule, adopted here verbatim: **a mid-hearing fix without a walker behind it must carry a control a judge can execute against the pre-fix tree** — «the builder watched it fail» is a claim, not a control. (The one fix that met this bar was verified by a judge checking out the pre-fix module and running both sides; that is the template.)

## Gap 5 — small laws from the same campaign, one line each

- **A Built quote must post-date the build it certifies** — a receipt quoting an earlier build's Built line is a relabel by omission.
- **The sibling question at every fix**: *what is this object's sibling field, this check's sibling check, this table's sibling table?* Asked once per fix, it would have prevented most of two hearings. Its two known blind spots, found by later readers: a projection's width, and a sentence's twin in another file.

## Gap 6 — the owner's named key re-sequences the map, now

The owner named it in writing, a day before the miss surfaced: the domain model is the key to the product's success and defensibility, to be nailed in tight collaboration (his verbatim in the private record). The conductor filed that round as «before the next piece» and spent the day perfecting machinery the round would have re-founded. The kernel's form, one sentence in the conductor law: **when the owner names something the key, it becomes the live work's dependency in the same session — re-sequenced out loud, or put back to him as a question — never a queued round.** «Before piece N+1» is where keys go to be lost.
