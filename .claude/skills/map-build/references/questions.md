# The mapping forks

Run as rounds — one or two at a time, options with costs and a recommendation, the owner's answers verbatim in `work/mapping.md`. Use what this product needs.

**Cutting the pieces:**

1. Reading the promises, moments, frames, and shaped material — what are the natural pieces, and what does each serve and consume? (Present the candidate cut; the owner reshapes it.)
2. Which shaped material has no piece? It goes on the unconsumed list, visibly — or the owner kills it now, on purpose.
3. Is any piece serving nothing in `product.md`? Then it's either a missing promise (re-enter shaping) or scope creep (kill it).

**Ordering — the real forks:**

4. Where does the first real surface arrive, and what does the owner want to see first with his own eyes? Machinery-first is legitimate when he chooses it knowing the cost: nothing visible until later, stated in the map.
5. Which ordering unlocks proof the earliest — the users, households, or data the later pieces need to be proven against? (A piece scheduled last can silently lock out the proof of every piece before it — that happened.)
6. What does each ordering cost if the direction changes after three pieces? Prefer orders that keep the expensive reversals late and the learning early.

**Proven, per piece:**

7. For each piece: what runs, which walks its witnesses live (the worst day included where it matters), and what its judge rules on. One line each, in the map.
8. Which pieces are risky-work (money, auth, private data, irreversible sends) and carry extra care from the start?

**Milestones:**

9. What is the smallest set of pieces that proves a real increment end to end — something the owner can drive and grade? Name it, and name the one after it.

**The substrate round (closes mapping):**

10. What do the pieces actually require of what they run on — data shape, model access, latency, privacy commitments, cost ceiling? Requirements first, brands last.
11. The options, with real costs: money per month, lock-in, ops burden, what reversal costs after N pieces. Include the boring option. The owner picks at a stated care level — and over-engineering the substrate past the stated care level is the recorded failure ("we went a bit overboard"), same defect as under-engineering it.
12. What would reopen the substrate decision? Write it into the decision entry.
