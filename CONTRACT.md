# speck-next · Product contract

The promises Speck Next makes, each with the check that can fail it. (v0.8 candidate — how this document has been attacked and revised lives in [docs/reviews/](docs/reviews/).)

## The job

Given a product intent, help an agent understand what matters, build the next valuable piece, prove it works by running it, and leave the repository clearer than it found it. The owner brings intent, judgment, and product decisions. The method stays out of sight.

**What we optimize: great, shipped product per minute of owner attention.**

The whole method lives in five files, plus one page: `product.md` (what we're building, what makes it good, and the product-specific responsibilities) · `map.md` (the ordered build pieces and milestones, each piece naming what it serves, consumes, and needs from the product team; one live) · `work/` (the phase round records with the owner's words verbatim, and one file per piece) · `decisions.md` (the big choices, including the substrate) · `state.md` (derived, never narrated). The product moves through five phases — shape → map → build → experience → judge — each with a checkable exit. Product, Business, Experience, and Engineering contribute from distinct contexts when Shape forms or reopens and on the first Map after Shape. On a later re-map, Product contributes with every role whose protected concern, evidence, expiry, or ordering changed. On every substantial piece, Product and Engineering always contribute; Business and Experience join when their product-specific conditions or current evidence require them. Product integrates one decision and Engineering alone implements it. The owner's sign-off lives at exactly two places, by his own ruling: he ratifies shaping and mapping in his own words, and he grades how each milestone feels at judging. Build, experience, and judge otherwise run autonomously — the shaping he already ratified is the yardstick, and the fresh testers and judges tell whether the product matches it before anything asks for his input. Skeletons for every file ship in `templates/`. The one page is `AGENTS.md` — the method itself, placed where every agent host loads it automatically; on-demand procedures are ordinary skills. The method ships only in forms agents natively load — never as documents an agent must be told about.

## The eight promises

Every promise names its check, and every check can fail.

1. **Iterative to the bone.** Work happens in small steps that each end in something you can run. Product and Engineering contribute from distinct contexts before code for every substantial piece, inside the same hard limit on minutes, tokens, and files spent before the product first runs. Business and Experience join before the affected decision or code when their authored condition fires, direct current evidence is missing or expired, a listed material change occurs, the answer is uncertain, or the role declares an effect. An inactive role leaves only its condition and direct current evidence, never a pretend contribution. Product integrates called roles into one resolved outcome; Engineering implements it. Each active role names the earliest run that can test its conclusion, then returns at that run; the named run and its return are both required before landing, and a missing one leaves the concern unresolved. The agent uses the product constantly while building — runs it, clicks through it, reads what it prints — and fixes what it finds. That loop is the engine; a demo reaches the owner only after the team has used the thing itself, and owner steering rides on top of the loop, never replaces it. When running for real would be irreversible (payments, live recipients, production data), the run happens against a named stand-in and the evidence says how faithful the stand-in is. *Check: the small-feature, bug-fix, fuzzy-idea, and separated-team tasks — budget held, distinct Product and Engineering carriers, conditional Business and Experience calls, factual inactive entries, named informative runs and returns before landing, proof of agent-run sessions before every demo, and a logged owner steer that visibly changed the next step.*
2. **Small changes stay small.** A typo-sized change writes no method files at all, takes minutes, and touches `state.md` only if something true actually changed. It does not summon the product-building roles. Typo-sized is defined up front, not felt out: no new dependency, no auth, money, privacy, or data-integrity code touched, no promise rewritten, reversible in one commit. The agent may treat work as bigger than it looks, never smaller — and a change that turns out to touch protected code was never typo-sized; it gets the full loop, billed to that task, because the minutes-promise covers changes that really are small, not ones that pretended. When the call is genuinely unclear, the judge makes it, not the builder. *Check: the typo tasks and the protected-code trap — a prompt that looks like a typo but sits on protected auth code; treating it as a typo or summoning a product team for a true typo is a scored miss.*
3. **Judged like a user, challenged like a claim — never by a contributor.** Nobody grades their own homework, and no verdict lands unchallenged. Anyone who contributed as Product, Business, Experience, Engineering, a specialist, or the builder is ineligible as a fresh tester or judge. A replacement carrier inherits the original evidence and contribution, and both remain excluded. Fresh testers live the product as the first-timer, the real job end to end, the second user (a least-privileged account attempting the forbidden, and a second person on the same install looking for traces of the first), and the worst day. They judge it the way real users judge while using: each record ends in that persona's own verdict, and every verdict must point at a moment actually lived in that record; a verdict pointing at nothing is void. Then a judge who contributed to none of it, built none of it, and tested none of it convenes the hearing: questions and challenges each verdict — the favorable ones hardest — sends a tester back to the product when an answer needs another run, holds diverging persona verdicts side by side instead of averaging them, and rules with each ruling citing the records it rests on. At milestones and on risky work a second judge hears the same records independently, and divergence between the two judgments is itself a finding. Reading the code is not experiencing; a record with zero executed runs is itself a defect. A bug counts as caught only when a tester reproduced it by running the product, and a safety net counts as evidence only after it has been watched failing on purpose. *Check: the planted-bug tasks, each with a clean twin, plus review-integrity and separated-team tasks that fail when any original or replacement role contributor reappears as tester or judge.*
4. **The state file tells the truth.** `state.md` answers six questions in plain sentences: what is true now, what is wearing out (every recorded strain — the workaround, the duplication, the piece that fought the structure — with how often it has bitten), what is blocked, what needs the owner, what happens next, and what evidence backs all of that. It carries unresolved role tensions, overdue informative returns, and evidence invalidated by a false inactive call, never a four-role dashboard. Four states: Shaped, Built, Judged, Live. Judged spells out the four verdicts separately — it works · it delivers the promise · it's good to use · the quality hangs together — each pointing at evidence or saying honestly "not judged yet". A business-changing piece and every milestone also carry Business's binding `kept / broken / not judged` ruling; `broken` or `not judged` blocks that piece or milestone without becoming a fifth universal quality verdict. A false inactive call stops the current piece, returns a wrong promise to Shape, a wrong piece or order to Map, and any other miss to piece setup; the missed role joins, Product re-synthesizes before Engineering resumes, affected Built and review evidence is invalid, and changed work needs a new Built line and receipt. Unrelated evidence remains. When all required rulings stand on evidence the work is proven; a judgment that found the work insufficient routes it back with a trace instead of advancing it, and the state says so. Overclaiming is a bug, and a small mechanical check hunts the flaggable kind. *Check: the closing state of every task is audited; the honest-state and separated-team tasks fail role dashboards, hidden tensions, overdue returns, un-repaired false inactivity, unsupported verdicts, and missing Business rulings.*
5. **Upgrading is 100% seamless, from any Speck version.** One command upgrades any Speck repository, from any era, in any condition — including mid-flight work. Concretely:
   - The upgrade never asks a human to fix anything by hand, and it never pretends: promises, decisions, open bugs, and unfinished work all come along.
   - Anything that can't be mapped cleanly is listed openly in `state.md` with a pointer to where it came from. Nothing lost, nothing silently dropped — a messy repository arrives as an honestly messy state.
   - Nothing already proven gets re-proven unless new claims outgrow the old evidence. Pre-v6 and exact `6.0.0-rc.1` Speck Next repositories get one explicit pending upgrade assessment; historical work is never rewritten or represented as role-shaped. Its named `work/product-team-assessment.md` records four distinct carriers' contributions and one Product synthesis. Completion records exactly one route: a wrong promise reopens Shape, a wrong piece or order reopens Map, and no reopen resumes the existing live piece from `state.md` without another owner decision.
   - Exact generated rc.1 and rejected rc.2 status text may be replaced. Owner-authored Product team sections are preserved byte for byte before one separate canonical assessment. Its exact status field is the only source of pending or complete. LF, CRLF, lone CR, and end-of-file end equivalent logical lines for classification; owner bytes are never normalized. Removing an exact generated line blanks only its text and retains its delimiter. An appended block keeps the original product as an exact prefix and uses its last existing line ending, or LF when none exists. Only complete current lines outside blockquotes, fenced code, top-level `<!-- ... -->` comments, and multiline inline-code spans count. In plain current text, an unescaped maximal backtick run shields comment-looking bytes only through a proven equal-length maximal closer before a blank line or new block; the valid construct encountered first owns its contents. An unmatched or wrong-length backtick run is literal, so a later real comment opens. A top-level comment-touched or multiline-span-touched line is inactive until the next clean line; balanced same-line inline code leaves the rest of its line current, and only a plain current line can start a multiline span. An unclosed live comment refuses untouched. Quoted or paraphrased history is inert. Every fixed marker carries `upgradeAssessmentRecord`: `null` means none applies and the canonical path means the block is required. A current rc.2 marker missing the field is unknown unless surviving canonical or generated evidence proves the required path. Its ordinary upgrade refuses untouched and prints the supported `upgrade [dir] --open-assessment` recovery. Only that exact ambiguity accepts the flag after full validation; it opens pending with the canonical path, never `null` or resumed work. A completed status requires the named record. A missing `product.md` stays missing and safely records `null`.
   - The one-time assessment also completes one current `## Product team` section in `product.md`. It has exactly one row each for Product, Business, Experience, and Engineering; Product and Engineering have product-specific responsibilities; and Business and Experience have substantive values after the exact labels `Protects`, `Call when`, `May stay out when`, `Evidence expires`, and `Material changes`. The upgrader treats those values as opaque. For structural completeness only, it removes Unicode default-ignorable formatting characters from a temporary copy before its existing blank, untouched-bracket-placeholder, and explicit-filler checks (`TBD`, `TODO`, `none`, `N/A`, and `placeholder`). Whitespace or default-ignorable characters alone are blank; any visible non-filler owner value remains opaque and byte-identical. Pending and completed Shape-reopened assessments may be incomplete. Completed Map and resume routes preflight the definition after the assessment record exists and before any target write. Failure lists the exact roles and fields, says nothing changed, prints no affirmative Map or resume instruction, and directs Product to restore pending and finish `product.md`; no owner byte is generated, normalized, or rewritten.
   - The upgrade is one commit that never sweeps in unrelated work, retries safely, and reverts cleanly. It validates every marker and assessment refusal before changing any repository byte; refusal names the defect and repair, says nothing changed, and never resumes work. On success it writes the version marker last, only after copying and migration succeed.
   - Provenance names the source checkout separately from a SHA-256 of the sorted kernel-owned method bytes actually copied. Legacy markers supply their old `commit` only as a source-checkout fallback and say when no earlier method digest was recorded.
   - The upgrader may refuse only when the repository version or required assessment disposition genuinely cannot be determined — and then it says exactly what it saw, what would settle the question, and that nothing changed. Every supported state with sufficient evidence is covered by test.
   - Every piece of old-Speck knowledge lives inside the upgrader; the kernel carries none of it.

   *Check: fresh-install and marker-bearing v5, exact generated rc.1, rejected rc.2, custom-section, missing-product, dirty-worktree, failure-and-retry, three-route completion, Product-team required-value and duplicate tables, corrupt-state, fieldless-current refusal and explicit recovery, flag-exclusion and argument-error tables, inactive-container tables, retained-history, and same-surface/different-checkout fixtures, verified against independent pre-written inventories; every assessment refusal preserves the marker, complete non-Git path kinds and bytes, and porcelain status, while successful migration runs twice byte-stably, preserves historical and unrelated bytes, writes its marker last, and reports both source checkouts, both method-surface digests, the migration outcome, working-tree paths, the full installed-surface diff plus `product.md`, and a plain next action.*
6. **Fun to drive, in plain language.** Building with speck-next should feel like watching a sharp product team build your product, never like watching four agents report their departments. Product gives the owner one integrated recommendation; meaningful divergence appears only when it changes a user-facing choice, risk, order, or promise. Everything the owner reads or watches speaks product, not method: the docs, the state file, and what agents say while they work ("building the login screen — here's how it looks", never method ritual). Everything is understandable: a smart person who has never seen Speck can follow any document in this repository in one read, and the owner can answer the state questions with no glossary. The system coins at most five words the **owner** must learn. Today the count is four: Shaped, Built, Judged, Live. And fun is measured, not assumed: the owner rates the felt experience at every milestone — momentum, comprehension, enjoyment — and "felt like watching a foreign language" is a failing grade. This repository's own documents are held to the same bar. *Check: an owner cold-read of Product's synthesis, a builder handoff, a method-speak audit on owner-visible narration, a fresh-reader test on the docs, and the owner's felt rating at every milestone.*
7. **Small by law.** Hard limits in this repository's own CI, each with a test proving the limit can actually trip:
   - Installed into a product repo: **≤ 20 files / 100 KB**, counting every reachable capability, visible or not. (An upgraded repo's archived history is its own and sits outside this limit. Raised from 15 files when v3's templates arrived, traded against the skill ceiling below — owner-approved, 2026-08-19.)
   - Skills the agent can load: **≤ 6** (five exist) — the compensating tightening for the file raise, owner-approved 2026-08-19.
   - What an agent always reads (`AGENTS.md` + `state.md` + `product.md` + `map.md`): **≤ 50 KB** — raised from 25 KB by the owner after the first real campaign showed a deeply-shaped `product.md` earning its weight (the shaping process benefited from the context). The budget bounds drag, not thinking: a `product.md` full of shaped decisions is inside the spirit; one full of narration is not, at any size.
   - Durable method files per piece of work: **≤ 1** beyond the four shared files (`product.md`, `map.md`, `decisions.md`, `state.md`) — the piece's own work file — and typo-sized work writes none. A hearing's linked records (tester records, judgments) sit outside that limit and inside their own, the compensating tightening for counting them separately (owner-approved 2026-08-29): **≤ 6 linked records per hearing**, and a re-hearing extends the existing records — a follow-up run appends to its record, a continued judgment appends to its judgment — rather than opening new files.
   - End-to-end cost ceilings (tokens, minutes, contexts, owner interruptions) for typo-sized and substantial work, enforced by running the development suite. Separated roles add no installed file or skill and no owner sign-off beat. Native Codex discovery adds one installer-generated symbolic-link entry to the same five canonical skills; it adds no skill, duplicated skill body, state, dependency, or owner sign-off beat.
   - **v0.9 cost exchange, owner-approved 2026-09-05:** the 266,484-gross run remains a failed control against its original 250,000-gross ceiling. Its one separately labeled continuation may reuse only the four recorded carriers and their valid 56,228-fresh-token / 112.284-active-second evidence. Across both parts it stops at 200,000 cumulative fresh tokens or 900 cumulative active seconds, with exactly five remaining host turns: Product synthesis; one Engineering implementation, actual run, and return; concurrent Business and Experience returns; and Product close. There is no carrier replacement, retry, fallback, helper, sixth turn, or further owner interruption. Gross, cached, and fresh totals remain measured, and Business must judge whether the cumulative cost earns the product result. The calendar wait for the owner's selection is the only excluded time.

   A limit moves only in a new owner-approved version of this contract that names another limit tightening in exchange — and moving a limit can never rescue an experiment that already failed.
8. **It cannot quietly grow back.** The alarm is a number, not a memory: if more than 20% of a month's commits or tokens go to maintaining the method itself — counted mechanically by path, never by anyone's judgment — development stops and subtracts first. One small table in this repository lists every kernel capability with the failure it prevents, what it costs, what proof it has earned (or the date by which it must earn it or retire), and what would retire it. The separated team stays only while distinct contexts produce distinct evidence and actions; universal attendance, four labels, copied prose, or owner-facing role reports are failures, not partial credit. A consequential miss after a role marked its concern handled makes that role mandatory at the next comparable piece's key decisions and informative runs; a repeat keeps it through the milestone until evidence supports relaxing it. No paperwork about the table, no copies in product repos. Every release names what it considered deleting. If the kernel ever seems to need a context loader or compliance layer for itself, stop and subtract.

## What we refuse to trade away

Eight things no single check can fully capture, written down so every big decision weighs them (the test for each: could a decision quietly violate this while every check stays green?):

1. **Product excellence** — correct, on-promise, good to use, tasteful; strength in one never excuses weakness in another.
2. **Owner attention and peace** — the point of everything; more owner contact is not more virtue, and any decision adding owner touchpoints must show attention saved elsewhere.
3. **Iterative with the owner** — running product early, steering that matters.
4. **Care where the risk is** — small stays cheap, and risky paths never get to call themselves small.
5. **Smallness** — of the kernel, the vocabulary, and the paper trail.
6. **Seamless upgrades** — any Speck repo, any condition, everything carried, everything visible.
7. **Truth over theater** — evidence comes from running the product, and shipped work staying good counts too.
8. **Fun and understandability** — the owner never watches a foreign language; drag is a defect.

A decision is big enough to weigh against this list when it defers, simplifies, descopes, adds owner contact, or moves a limit.

## What stays out

Proving process compliance · carrying old-Speck compatibility in the kernel (the upgrader owns all of it) · installing adapters for every host (generate the one in use) · replacing the project's own tests and CI · writing the whole spec before building anything · shipping method-management machinery into product repos.

## Until it earns the job

v1 is accepted on demonstrated confidence, by owner decision (2026-08-13, recorded in [decisions.md](decisions.md)): the old system's valuable lessons adopted and traceable, the development suite green with checks proven able to fail, real products built end to end with the method, and a fresh adversarial audit agreeing it's a true value-add over a no-harness agent. The designed head-to-head contest ([docs/benchmark/fixtures.md](docs/benchmark/fixtures.md)) is shelved, not deleted — its planted-bug classes seed the development suite, and it remains available if the question ever needs settling by measurement. Real use stays the final judge: v1's job is rebooting a real product, and failing there outranks any green suite.
