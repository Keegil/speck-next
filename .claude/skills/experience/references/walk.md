# Walk a user interface

Use the product as your assigned person. Record the run as it happens. End with three separate verdicts: does it work, feel good, and look crafted? Each verdict points to your own screenshots and moments so the judge can challenge it.

1. Read the promise, magic moments, declared feel, and restraint rules. If no feel was declared, say that your craft verdict can use only general craft.
2. Run the real build at a named commit, not the dev server. Start cold from a fresh install or cleared storage, logged out, on a realistic screen.
3. At the first screen, forget the brief. From the pixels alone, answer: What is this? Who is asking? Why now? Why should I care? Record confusion as you feel it.
4. If you cannot enter, reproduce and record the real barrier first. Then unblock honestly with a throwaway account or test route, without faking what you are evaluating.
5. Complete the whole job. Capture every screen and meaningful state: loading, empty, error, and success. Continue after a break because later defects often hide behind the first.
6. Trace each claim. When the product says it saved, sent, or generated something, find the request, changed record, and read-back. Record what you found or could not find.
7. Behave badly. Go back mid-flow, background the app, enter garbage, cut the network, and force an error. Record recovery step by step, including what was lost. Sign in as a second person on the same install and look for traces of the first.
8. Open every screenshot. Write at least two observations pointing to its pixels. “No issues” needs evidence; an unexamined screenshot proves nothing.
9. Run accessibility and console checks. Keep raw output for contrast, focus order, touch targets, text scaling, and keyboard overlap.
10. Live every promised moment separately. Did its trigger fire? Did its beats happen? Did it feel as promised or merely occur? Answer in persona.
11. Inspect the same screenshots for craft. Point to hierarchy, spacing, color, type, motion, copy, and emotional tone. Ask how the screen was made, not only whether it is clear.
12. End with your three verdicts: works, feels good, and crafted. Write as this person and cite the moments behind each claim. The judge’s four final rulings are separate; do not copy them.

## Judge taste without imposing your own

Compare the product with its declared feel, not a universal style. Put the declared feel beside what you felt.

When a named, checkable rule is broken, quote the rule and location together. That is an objective finding. Record a mood mismatch as your felt response for the owner’s taste call. Do not disguise your taste as an objective rule or quietly redesign toward it.

Inspect every screen for clipped or overlapping elements, unreachable primary actions, too many type sizes, accents doing unrelated jobs, ragged alignment, placeholder copy, and a mood that fights the declaration. List exact examples.

If automation cannot reach a control, record that as its own observation. It may also be unreachable to assistive technology.

When taste genuinely forks, give the owner the real choices and the cost of each. Three meaningful forks beat thirty.

## Return a useful record

Include the commit and real build; an examined screenshot for every screen and state; raw accessibility output; timings when speed shapes the feel; the mechanism behind each claimed action; a critique per screen; anything untested or unreachable; owner taste choices; and the three evidence-linked verdicts. Leave out ceremony.
